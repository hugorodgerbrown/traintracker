Use it from Claude or ChatGPT

Traintrackr is a connector: you add it once, and your AI chat uses it when you ask about trains. In Claude, add it from the connector directory; anywhere else, add it by its address.

Claude

Traintrackr is listed in Claude's connector directory. It is free, and works on every Claude plan.

  1. Open Traintrackr in Claude's connector directory. Or, on claude.ai or in the Claude desktop app, open Customize, then Connectors, choose +, then Browse connectors, and search for Traintrackr.
  2. Choose Connect. Claude opens the Traintrackr sign-in page: enter your email address, then the six-digit code sent to it.
  3. Start a new chat and ask about a train. If Claude doesn't use Traintrackr, open the + menu under the message box, choose Connectors and turn Traintrackr on.

A connector added on claude.ai also works in the desktop and mobile apps. To add it by address instead, choose +, then Add custom connector, name it Traintrackr and paste the address below.

ChatGPT

ChatGPT adds connectors through developer mode, on the web only. Business, Enterprise and Edu plans can use every tool; Pro can use only the ones that read, which is all of Traintrackr's. In a workspace, an admin turns developer mode on, or allows you to.

  1. In ChatGPT on the web, open Settings, then Apps, then Advanced settings, and turn on Developer mode.
  2. Back in Apps, choose Create. Give it the name Traintrackr, paste the address and choose OAuth.
  3. Choose Scan Tools. ChatGPT opens the Traintrackr sign-in page: enter your email address, then the six-digit code sent to it. Then choose Create.
  4. Traintrackr is now under Settings, Apps, marked Dev. Start a new chat and ask.

There is no password: each sign-in sends a six-digit code that lasts 15 minutes and works once. Traintrackr sees only the stations and times your chat asks about, never the conversation; the privacy policy says what is kept. To disconnect, remove the connector in your AI chat's settings.

Worked examples

1. Which platform?

Which platform is the next train from Cambridge to Kings Cross?

The assistant uses Departure platform. The answer contains:

  • the train: its booked departure time, destination and operator;
  • the platform, and whether it is live (announced) or booked (from the timetable, and able to change);
  • the minutes until it leaves, and the expected time if it is late;
  • a note if the train is cancelled or the platform is not yet announced.

Large stations often announce a platform about 10 minutes before departure. Until then you get the booked platform, marked as such.

2. Live departures

What are the next trains from Liverpool Street to Colchester, and are they on time?

The assistant uses Live departures. The answer contains, for each train in the next two hours:

  • booked and expected departure times;
  • its status: on time, late and by how many minutes, or cancelled;
  • the reason for a delay or cancellation, where one is given;
  • the platform, the operator and the destination;
  • any notices posted for the station.

Ask to see the board (show me the departures board for Liverpool Street) and, in an app that supports it, the assistant uses Show a departure board to draw the same trains as a departure board. Its Refresh button asks for the board again.

3. Planning a journey

How do I get from Cambridge to Sudbury on Saturday morning?

The assistant uses Plan a journey. The answer contains:

  • three options unless you ask for more, fastest first, and always the one with the fewest changes;
  • for each option: departure and arrival times, the time it takes and the number of changes;
  • for each leg: the train, where to board and where to get off, and the booked platform where known;
  • for trains leaving today within two hours: live times, and a warning if a delay puts a connection at risk.

Changes between London terminals are included as estimated walking or Tube links.

Tools

An assistant chooses the tool; you don't need to name one. Every tool only reads data, and takes station names or three-letter station codes.

Find a station find_station
Matches a name, part of a name or a station code to stations. Used when a name fits several, such as Sudbury.
Live departures live_departures
The departure board for the next two hours: expected times, platforms, delays and cancellations.
Live arrivals live_arrivals
The arrivals board for the next two hours.
Show a departure board show_board
The same departures or arrivals, drawn on screen as a departure board. Used when you ask to see a board.
Departure platform departure_platform
The platform for one train: the next departure, or the one booked at a time you give.
Departures from a platform platform_departures
The next trains to leave from one platform.
Timetable timetable
Booked departures or arrivals at a station on any date the timetable covers, usually months ahead.
Service details service_details
Every stop for one train, with live times where there are any.
Plan a journey plan_journey
Journeys between two stations, with changes.
Data status data_status
Which data sources are working and how fresh the timetable is.
Privacy policy privacy_policy
The privacy policy, so your assistant can answer questions about what is kept about you.

Limits

  • Live data covers the next two hours. Beyond that, times are booked times from the timetable.
  • No past running times. Traintrackr can't say whether yesterday's 08:00 was late.
  • No fares.
  • No Tube, bus or tram, apart from the estimated links between London terminals.
  • Great Britain only: National Rail services. Northern Ireland is not covered.
  • Engineering works appear in the timetable once Network Rail publishes them, and in live data on the day.
  • Bank holidays: the timetable doesn't yet apply bank-holiday running, so check the live board on the day.
  • When live data is unavailable, the boards show booked times and say so.
  • Fair use. {{ fair_use }}

Check with your train operator before you travel if a journey matters.

Where the data comes from

  • Live times, platforms, delays and cancellations come from Darwin, National Rail Enquiries' real-time system, through the Rail Data Marketplace. Powered by National Rail Enquiries.
  • The timetable comes from Network Rail Infrastructure Limited's SCHEDULE data feed and is refreshed each day. Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0.
  • The station list comes from davwheat/uk-railway-stations, under the Open Database License.
